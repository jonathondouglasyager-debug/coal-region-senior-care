# Assisted Living vs. Nursing Home: What's the Difference?

When a loved one needs more support than they can manage at home, families in the Coal Region face one of the most important decisions they'll ever make: assisted living or a nursing home?

These two options are often confused, but they serve very different needs. Understanding the distinction can save your family thousands of dollars, reduce stress, and most importantly, help your loved one get the right level of care.

## What Is Assisted Living?

In Pennsylvania, assisted living facilities are called **Personal Care Homes**. They provide:

- Help with daily activities like bathing, dressing, and medication management
- Meals, housekeeping, and laundry services
- Social activities and community engagement
- A private or semi-private room in a residential setting
- Staff available around the clock, but not 24/7 skilled nursing

Residents in assisted living generally manage most of their own care but need help with certain tasks. They can typically walk (with or without aids), feed themselves, and make their own decisions.

**In Schuylkill County**, assisted living options include Providence Place in Pottsville, Luther Ridge at Seiders Hill, Schoolyard Square in Pine Grove, and Heritage Mills in Tower City.

## What Is a Nursing Home?

Nursing homes — formally called **Skilled Nursing Facilities (SNFs)** — provide:

- 24-hour skilled nursing care from licensed nurses
- Medical care supervised by physicians
- Rehabilitation services (physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy)
- Help with all daily activities
- Specialized care for complex medical conditions

Nursing home residents typically need a level of medical care that cannot be safely provided in an assisted living setting. Many residents require help with mobility, wound care, IV medications, or ventilator support.

**In Schuylkill County**, nursing homes include Schuylkill Center, Rosewood Rehabilitation and Nursing Center in Schuylkill Haven, and Seton Manor in Orwigsburg, among others.

## Key Differences at a Glance

**Level of care:** Assisted living provides personal care support. Nursing homes provide skilled medical care.

**Staffing:** Assisted living has caregivers and may have a nurse on staff. Nursing homes have licensed nurses on duty 24/7.

**Cost:** In Pennsylvania, assisted living averages $4,000–$6,000 per month. Nursing homes average $9,000–$12,000 per month for a semi-private room.

**Independence:** Assisted living residents maintain more independence and autonomy. Nursing home residents receive more structured, medically-oriented care.

**Regulation:** In PA, personal care homes are licensed by the Department of Human Services. Nursing homes are licensed by the Department of Health and regulated by federal Medicare/Medicaid standards.

## How to Decide Which Is Right

Ask yourself these questions:

1. **Does your loved one need skilled medical care?** If they need wound care, IV therapy, or complex medication management, a nursing home is likely necessary.

2. **Can they manage most daily activities with some help?** If they mainly need help with bathing, dressing, or medication reminders, assisted living may be sufficient.

3. **Is the need temporary or long-term?** Some nursing homes specialize in short-term rehabilitation after a hospital stay, with the goal of returning home.

4. **What's your budget?** Assisted living is roughly half the cost of a nursing home. Medicare generally does not cover assisted living but may cover short-term skilled nursing.

5. **What does your loved one want?** Their preferences matter. Some people thrive in the social environment of assisted living. Others need the security of knowing medical staff is always nearby.

## A Note About Memory Care

If your loved one has Alzheimer's or another form of dementia, some assisted living facilities offer **memory care units** — secure environments with specialized programming. In Schuylkill County, Providence Place and Heritage Mills both offer memory care services.

For advanced dementia requiring skilled nursing, a nursing home with a memory care wing may be more appropriate.

## What to Do Next

1. **Talk to your loved one's doctor.** They can help assess the level of care needed.
2. **Visit facilities in person.** Nothing replaces seeing a place with your own eyes and talking to staff and residents.
3. **Ask about costs and payment options.** See our guide on [How to Pay for Senior Care in Pennsylvania](/guides/paying-for-senior-care-pa/).
4. **Browse our directory** to find [assisted living](/assisted-living/) and [nursing homes](/nursing-homes/) in Schuylkill County.

The right choice depends on your loved one's specific needs, and those needs may change over time. Many families start with assisted living and transition to a nursing home later if medical needs increase. That's not a failure — it's part of the journey.
