# How to Choose a Home Health Agency in Pennsylvania

Home health care lets your loved one receive skilled medical care or personal assistance in the comfort of their own home. For many Coal Region families, this is the preferred option — aging in the house where they've lived for decades, surrounded by what's familiar.

But not all home health agencies are the same. Choosing the right one matters.

## Types of Home Health Services

Home health agencies in Pennsylvania generally offer two types of care:

**Skilled home health care** includes services provided by licensed professionals:
- Registered nurses for wound care, medication management, and health monitoring
- Physical therapists for mobility and strength
- Occupational therapists for daily living skills
- Speech therapists for swallowing or communication issues
- Home health aides for personal care under nurse supervision

**Non-medical home care** includes:
- Companionship and supervision
- Meal preparation and light housekeeping
- Transportation to appointments
- Medication reminders (but not administration)
- Personal care like bathing and dressing

Some agencies offer both. Others specialize in one or the other. Make sure the agency you choose provides the specific services your loved one needs.

## Check for Medicare Certification

If your loved one is covered by Medicare, the home health agency must be **Medicare-certified** to bill Medicare for services. Medicare-certified agencies are also held to higher federal quality standards and undergo regular inspections.

In Schuylkill County, Medicare-certified agencies include Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Lehigh Valley Home Care, Advantage Home Health, and others listed in our [home health directory](/home-health/).

You can verify an agency's Medicare certification at [Medicare.gov's Home Health Compare](https://www.medicare.gov/care-compare/).

## Questions to Ask Before You Hire

When evaluating a home health agency, ask:

1. **Are you licensed by the state of Pennsylvania?** All home health agencies must be licensed by the PA Department of Health.
2. **Are you Medicare/Medicaid certified?** This matters for payment and quality standards.
3. **What services do you provide?** Make sure they cover what your loved one needs.
4. **How do you screen and train your staff?** Ask about background checks, training requirements, and ongoing supervision.
5. **Who develops the care plan?** A good agency involves the patient, family, and physician in care planning.
6. **What happens in an emergency?** Is there a nurse on call 24/7?
7. **How do you handle complaints?** Ask about their grievance process.
8. **Can you provide references?** Talk to other families who have used the agency.
9. **What are your rates?** If services aren't covered by insurance, understand the full cost upfront.
10. **How quickly can you start?** Some families need care immediately after a hospital discharge.

## Red Flags to Watch For

Be cautious if an agency:

- **Cannot provide proof of licensure or certification.** This is non-negotiable.
- **Has high staff turnover.** Ask how long their caregivers have been with the agency. Frequent turnover means inconsistent care.
- **Doesn't involve you in care planning.** Your family should be part of every major decision.
- **Won't let you meet the caregiver before service starts.** You should know who is coming into your loved one's home.
- **Pressures you into signing quickly.** A reputable agency will give you time to decide.
- **Has complaints filed with the PA Department of Health.** You can check complaint history through the department.

## What Medicare Covers

Medicare covers home health care if all of these are true:
- Your loved one is homebound (leaving home is a major effort)
- They need skilled nursing or therapy on an intermittent basis
- A doctor orders the care and certifies the need
- The agency is Medicare-certified

Medicare does **not** cover:
- 24-hour home care
- Personal care only (without a skilled need)
- Meals delivered to the home
- Homemaker services (cleaning, laundry) unless part of a skilled care plan

## Local Resources

Schuylkill County has both independent and health-system-affiliated agencies. Local agencies like Schuylkill Home Care (in business since 1988) offer deep community roots. Health system agencies like Lehigh Valley Home Care bring the backing of a large medical network.

The best choice depends on your specific situation. Browse our [home health directory](/home-health/) to compare options, and don't hesitate to call several agencies before deciding.

Your loved one deserves a caregiver they trust in the home they love.
