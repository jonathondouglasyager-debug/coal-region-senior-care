# Caregiver Burnout: Resources for Coal Region Families

You didn't plan to become a caregiver. Maybe it started with driving your mom to appointments. Then managing her medications. Then bathing her. Then being afraid to leave the house because what if something happens while you're gone.

If this sounds familiar, you're not alone. An estimated 53 million Americans provide unpaid care to an adult family member. In rural communities like the Coal Region, where families are close-knit and "we take care of our own" runs deep, the burden often falls on one or two family members — usually a daughter or daughter-in-law — without enough help.

This guide is for you.

## Recognizing Caregiver Burnout

Burnout doesn't happen overnight. It builds gradually, and it's easy to dismiss the signs because you're focused on your loved one's needs, not your own. Watch for:

**Physical signs:**
- Exhaustion that doesn't improve with rest
- Getting sick more often
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Trouble sleeping, even when you have the chance
- Neglecting your own medical appointments

**Emotional signs:**
- Feeling overwhelmed, helpless, or hopeless
- Resentment toward the person you're caring for (followed by guilt for feeling that way)
- Withdrawing from friends and activities you used to enjoy
- Irritability or mood swings
- Crying more than usual
- Feeling like caregiving is all there is to your life

**Behavioral signs:**
- Drinking more or using substances to cope
- Losing interest in things that used to bring you joy
- Snapping at your loved one or other family members
- Fantasizing about "escape" — even just wanting to drive and not come back

If you recognize yourself in this list, please keep reading. These feelings don't make you a bad person. They make you human.

## You Cannot Pour From an Empty Cup

This isn't just a saying. Research consistently shows that caregiver burnout leads to worse outcomes for both the caregiver and the person receiving care. Caregivers with burnout have higher rates of depression, heart disease, and weakened immune systems. And when you're running on empty, the quality of care you provide suffers, too.

Taking care of yourself isn't selfish. It's necessary.

## Local Resources in Schuylkill County

### Respite Care

Respite care gives you a break — a few hours, a day, or even a week — while someone else cares for your loved one.

- **Schuylkill County Office of Senior Services** coordinates respite care through the OPTIONS program. Call (570) 622-3103.
- **Adult day centers** like Avenues Eldergarten in Pottsville, Tamaqua Area Adult Day Care, and Holy Family Adult Day Care in Orwigsburg provide daytime supervision and activities so you can work, run errands, or simply rest.
- **Medicare hospice benefit** includes up to 5 days of inpatient respite care per benefit period, giving home hospice caregivers time to recharge.

### Support Groups

Talking to other people who understand what you're going through can be a lifeline.

- **Diakon Community Services** offers caregiver support programming. Call (570) 624-3010.
- **LIFE Geisinger** in Minersville provides support for families of PACE program participants. Call (570) 984-2051.
- **Alzheimer's Association** maintains a 24/7 helpline at 1-800-272-3900 and connects families with local support groups.
- **Area Agency on Aging** can connect you with caregiver support groups in Schuylkill County. Call (570) 622-3103.

### Home Care Assistance

Bringing in professional help — even a few hours a week — can make a significant difference.

- Browse our [home health directory](/home-health/) for agencies serving Schuylkill County
- Non-medical home care agencies like Schuylkill Home Care and Comfort Keepers can provide companion care, personal care, and household help
- Medicaid waiver programs may cover home care for eligible individuals

### Mental Health Support

If burnout has crossed into depression or anxiety, professional help is available:

- **Schuylkill County Crisis Line:** (570) 622-6697
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** Call or text 988 for free, confidential support
- **Your primary care doctor** can screen for depression and connect you with resources

## Practical Steps You Can Take Today

1. **Ask for help.** Call the Schuylkill County Office of Senior Services at (570) 622-3103 and ask about respite care, home-delivered meals, and other support programs.

2. **Accept imperfection.** Your house doesn't have to be spotless. Your loved one's care doesn't have to be perfect. Good enough is good enough.

3. **Set boundaries.** It's okay to say "I can't do this alone" to siblings, relatives, or the person you're caring for. Be specific about what you need.

4. **Keep one thing for yourself.** Whether it's a walk, a cup of coffee with a friend, or an hour of watching something you enjoy — protect that time.

5. **Plan for the long term.** If caregiving is unsustainable, start exploring [assisted living](/assisted-living/), [nursing homes](/nursing-homes/), or increased [home health](/home-health/) support. This isn't failure. This is planning.

## A Note About Guilt

Many caregivers feel guilty about needing a break, about getting frustrated, about considering a care facility, about not being able to do it all. Coal Region families especially carry a strong ethic of "we handle things ourselves."

But here's the truth: the best caregivers are the ones who know their limits. The families who reach out for help — who use respite care, who attend support groups, who bring in professional assistance — provide better care, for longer, with less damage to their own health.

You are doing something incredibly hard. Give yourself the same compassion you give your loved one.

If you need help right now, call the Schuylkill County Office of Senior Services at **(570) 622-3103**.
