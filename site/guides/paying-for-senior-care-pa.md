# How to Pay for Senior Care in Pennsylvania

Senior care is expensive. Whether it's assisted living at $4,000–$6,000 per month, a nursing home at $9,000–$12,000 per month, or home health care at $25–$35 per hour, the costs add up quickly. For many Coal Region families, figuring out how to pay is just as stressful as choosing a provider.

The good news: there are more options than most families realize. Here's a practical guide to what's available in Pennsylvania.

## Medicare

**What it covers:** Medicare covers short-term skilled care — not long-term care.
- Up to 100 days in a skilled nursing facility after a qualifying hospital stay (3+ days)
- Home health care if you're homebound and need skilled nursing or therapy
- Hospice care for terminal illness
- Durable medical equipment (walkers, wheelchairs, hospital beds)

**What it doesn't cover:** Assisted living, long-term nursing home stays, custodial care (help with bathing, dressing), or 24-hour home care.

**Key point:** Medicare is not designed for long-term care. It covers recovery and rehabilitation, not ongoing support.

## Medicaid (MA) in Pennsylvania

Medicaid is the primary payer for long-term care in Pennsylvania. It covers:
- Nursing home care for eligible residents
- Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waiver programs
- Personal care services in the home

**Eligibility:** Medicaid is based on income and assets. In Pennsylvania, an individual generally must have:
- Income at or below the special income level (approximately $2,829/month in 2026)
- Countable assets under $2,000 for an individual
- A spouse at home can keep additional assets and income (Community Spouse Resource Allowance)

**Important:** Pennsylvania has a "look-back period" of 60 months (5 years). If you transferred assets to qualify for Medicaid, there may be a penalty period during which Medicaid won't cover nursing home costs. This is why **planning ahead with an elder law attorney is critical.**

**How to apply:** Contact the Schuylkill County Assistance Office at (570) 621-3000 or apply online through COMPASS (Pennsylvania's online benefits portal).

## PA-Specific Programs

Pennsylvania offers several programs that help seniors stay at home or access care:

**OPTIONS Program:** Run by your local Area Agency on Aging (Schuylkill County Office of Senior Services), OPTIONS helps seniors who need care but don't qualify for Medicaid. Services include personal care, home-delivered meals, adult day care, and respite care. Call (570) 622-3103.

**LIFE Program (Living Independently for Elders):** Pennsylvania's version of PACE (Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly). LIFE Geisinger operates in Minersville and provides comprehensive medical and support services for people 55+ who qualify for nursing home care but want to stay home. Services include medical care, therapies, medications, meals, transportation, and personal care — all coordinated by one team. Call (570) 984-2051.

**PA MEDI (Medicare Education and Decision Insight):** Free health insurance counseling for seniors. Volunteers help you understand Medicare, compare plans, and resolve billing issues. Available through Diakon Community Services in Schuylkill County at (570) 624-3010.

**Property Tax/Rent Rebate Program:** Pennsylvanians 65+ with household income under $45,000 may qualify for rebates of up to $1,000 on property taxes or rent paid.

## VA Benefits

If your loved one is a veteran or the surviving spouse of a veteran, additional benefits may be available:

**VA Aid and Attendance:** A monthly pension supplement for veterans who need help with daily activities. This can help pay for assisted living, nursing home care, or in-home care. The benefit can add $1,000–$2,000+ per month depending on the veteran's status.

**VA Home Health Care:** The VA provides home health aides, skilled nursing, and therapies for enrolled veterans.

**State Veterans Homes:** Pennsylvania operates six state veterans homes providing nursing care at reduced costs for eligible veterans. The nearest to Schuylkill County are in Scranton and Lebanon.

**How to apply:** Contact the Schuylkill County Veterans Affairs office or call the VA at 1-800-827-1000.

## Long-Term Care Insurance

If your loved one purchased long-term care insurance earlier in life, it can help cover:
- Nursing home costs
- Assisted living costs
- Home health care
- Adult day care

**Check the policy carefully.** Benefits vary widely. Look at daily/monthly benefit amounts, benefit period (2 years? 5 years? lifetime?), elimination period (how many days you pay before coverage kicks in), and what triggers benefits.

If your loved one doesn't have long-term care insurance, it's generally too late to purchase it once care is needed. But for younger family members, it's worth considering.

## Private Pay and Other Options

- **Personal savings and retirement funds** are often the first source families draw from
- **Reverse mortgages** allow homeowners 62+ to convert home equity into cash
- **Life insurance conversion:** Some life insurance policies can be converted to help pay for care
- **Family contributions:** Many Coal Region families share costs among siblings and relatives
